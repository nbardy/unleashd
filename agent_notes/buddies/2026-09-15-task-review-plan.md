## Scope and Worker terminology

The current core defines a Worker as an ordinary Buddy in temporary Worker mode, with exactly one parent manager relationship; it can receive several related Tasks. Its own identity/memory persists across those Tasks, and its conversations hold execution history. An ephemeral harness sub-agent is a different mechanism. The owner's question about temporary identity requests clarification; it does not by itself approve or reject a redesign. This plan retains the current model and adds clear terminology to affected owner-facing help/docs without introducing a new lifetime or controller. Staffing or automatic-retirement changes require a separate concrete workflow decision.

## Decision history and evidence

Decision-maker: owner for planning-before-work; assistant for decomposition/order and proposed dispositions. No owner approval of every detailed mechanism is implied. Successor to the earlier efficiency plan and progress review; earlier files remain unchanged.

Why retain the six children: payload size, cursor correctness, invocation configuration, usage attribution, attention scheduling and composition have distinct proof obligations. Why add one child: the final restart patch is verified locally but absent from the earlier main delivery, and needs a bounded integration handoff. Why keep old records: later fixes do not retroactively turn failed attempts into successful ones, and broad historical checklists contain both valid invariants and obsolete mechanisms.

Tradeoff: separate acceptance adds modest coordination overhead. Avoid duplicated implementation by inspecting the changed artifact once and linking its evidence to the relevant criteria. Revisit task separation if two slices share one coherent small patch, or if a stable current build proves a remaining task already complete. A question about Worker identity is not sufficient evidence to reverse the core design.
